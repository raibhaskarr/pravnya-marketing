When your child has a disability, you will hear about many schemes, cards and certificates. It is easy to feel lost. This guide is a map of government schemes for children with disabilities in India: what each kind of support is for, who it is for, and where to start. It covers India only. Families in the UAE have a different system and should ask their emirate's authorities and their child's school.

Rules, amounts and websites change. Everything below is correct to the best of our knowledge at the time of writing, and each section points to the official source so you can check before you apply.

## Start here: the disability certificate and UDID card

Most support depends on a disability certificate, issued by a government medical authority after an assessment. The Unique Disability ID (UDID) card is the national identity card for persons with disabilities and is applied for on the official Swavlamban portal ([swavlambancard.gov.in](https://www.swavlambancard.gov.in)). Read our step-by-step guide: [UDID card for your child: how to apply](/blog/udid-card-for-your-child).

## The law behind it: the RPwD Act 2016

The Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 recognises 21 disabilities. It also defines a **benchmark disability** as at least 40% of a specified disability, and many entitlements are only for children with a benchmark disability. That is why the percentage on the certificate matters.

For children, the Act gives those with benchmark disabilities the right to free education from 6 to 18 years, and it requires government and government-recognised institutions to provide inclusive education.

## Early identification and therapy: RBSK and DEICs

Rashtriya Bal Swasthya Karyakram (RBSK) screens newborns and young children for a set of conditions, including developmental delays and disabilities. Children who need follow-up are referred to District Early Intervention Centres (DEICs), which can offer assessments and therapy. Ask your nearest government hospital or anganwadi about the DEIC for your district.

## Aids, hearing aids and cochlear implants: the ADIP scheme

The ADIP scheme helps people with disabilities buy aids and appliances, such as hearing aids, and in some cases cochlear implants. Eligibility includes a disability certificate of at least 40% and a family income limit. At the time of writing, the official material sets a ceiling of Rs 7 lakh per child for a cochlear implant with therapy for pre-lingual hearing loss between 1 and 5 years, and Rs 6 lakh for acquired hearing loss between 5 and 18 years. Check current rules on the official ADIP site ([adip.depwd.gov.in](https://adip.depwd.gov.in)).

## Health insurance for children with disabilities: Niramaya

Niramaya is a health insurance scheme run by the National Trust for people with autism, cerebral palsy, intellectual disability and multiple disabilities. It offers cover of up to Rs 1 lakh on a reimbursement basis, with the same cover whichever of these disabilities a person has. You enrol through an organisation registered with the National Trust. Check premiums and current rules on the National Trust website ([thenationaltrust.gov.in](https://thenationaltrust.gov.in)).

## School support for children with disabilities in India

Inclusive education for children with disabilities is supported under Samagra Shiksha. In practice, support depends on your school and state. Start by asking the school how it plans support for your child, and ask your Block Resource Centre about special educators and resources available in your area. If the school plans support through an IEP, read [what an IEP is](/blog/what-is-an-iep), and use our guide to [preparing for an IEP meeting](/blog/preparing-for-an-iep-meeting) to go in with clear notes.

## Disability scholarships and state schemes

Many scholarships and benefits are run by states, and they differ. Check your state's social welfare or disability department, and the National Scholarship Portal for central scholarships. A disability certificate or UDID card is usually needed.

## A simple checklist

Keep these ready, as originals and copies:

- Your child's birth certificate and Aadhaar
- Recent passport-size photos
- Medical and therapy reports, with dates
- The disability certificate and UDID card, once you have them
- Proof of address and, where asked, income certificate

Keeping these in one place saves time at every office visit. Pravnya can hold your child's reports and notes in one record, alongside their goals.

## Common questions

### Which document should I get first?

Usually the disability certificate and UDID card, because most schemes ask for them. You apply on the official Swavlamban portal.

### What is a benchmark disability?

Under the RPwD Act 2016, it means at least 40% of a specified disability. Many benefits are only for children with a benchmark disability.

### Is there a government scheme for cochlear implants?

Yes. The ADIP scheme can support a cochlear implant with therapy for eligible children, subject to a disability certificate of at least 40% and a family income limit. Check current rules on adip.depwd.gov.in.

### Is there health insurance for children with autism or cerebral palsy?

Niramaya, run by the National Trust, offers cover of up to Rs 1 lakh for autism, cerebral palsy, intellectual disability and multiple disabilities. Check current details on the National Trust website.

### Do these schemes apply in the UAE?

No. These are Indian schemes. Families in the UAE should ask their emirate's authorities and their child's school about support.
